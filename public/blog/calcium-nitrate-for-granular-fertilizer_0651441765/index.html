Understanding the root causes is paramount for procurement professionals seeking to mitigate this problem and ensure consistent product performance. The fundamental issue centers on the hygroscopic nature of calcium nitrate (Ca(NO₃)₂), where its propensity to readily absorb atmospheric moisture leads to crystal growth and the formation of hard, clumpy masses. This phenomenon is exacerbated in environments with high relative humidity, common throughout the United States during periods of elevated precipitation. Mitigation Strategies: Optimizing Storage and Handling Practices

Caking in granular calcium nitrate agriculture fertilizer formulations, a prevalent issue across the United States, dramatically impacts product usability, storage efficiency, and ultimately, the American farmer’s return on investment. This section details mitigation strategies focusing on proactive measures implemented during sourcing, storage, and handling. Caking, characterized by the formation of hard, clumpy masses, is predominantly observed when calcium nitrate is exposed to elevated humidity levels, particularly during transport and warehousing. Analysis of formulations suggests that excessive moisture combined with elevated temperatures – frequently exceeding 85°F (29°C) – accelerates crystallization of the nitrate salts. Addressing this necessitates a multi-faceted approach incorporating rigorous quality control and optimized logistics. Procurement professionals should focus on securing calcium nitrate fertilizers from reputable manufacturers adhering to stringent quality control standards. Specifically, we recommend specifying a product with a guaranteed minimum of 15.5% nitrogen5 to ensure consistent nutrient delivery. Furthermore, detailed moisture content specifications – ideally below 1.5% – within the product’s technical data sheet are critical. During transport, utilizing temperature-controlled containers and minimizing exposure to direct sunlight are vital. Storage facilities must maintain a consistent temperature below 75°F (24°C) and a relative humidity level below 60% to prevent caking. Proper palletization, utilizing breathable pallets and avoiding stacking containers directly on the warehouse floor, further reduces the risk. Regular inspection and monitoring of stored inventory, coupled with prompt removal of any caked material for reprocessing or disposal, is a key operational element.
