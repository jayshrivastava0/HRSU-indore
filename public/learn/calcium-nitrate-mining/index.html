Calcium nitrate in mining and tailings: what the evidence supports

Last updated: 14 October 2026
In summary

Calcium nitrate has documented uses that matter to mining operations in two areas: cold-weather concreting for surface structures and ground works, and nitrate dosing to control sulfide in water systems. For tailings specifically (dust control, heavy-metal immobilisation, acid rock drainage) we found no peer-reviewed evidence for calcium nitrate, so HRSU makes no claim there. This page separates what is documented from what is not.

Key facts at a glance

QuestionWhat the evidence says
Cold-weather set accelerationIn a 2001 comparison of calcium salts at equimolar doses, calcium nitrate was the most effective set accelerator at lower temperatures, and at the lowest test temperature (5 °C) it outperformed calcium chloride.[1]

Where calcium nitrate is documented around mining operations

  • Cold-weather concreting for surface works. Foundations, portal structures, plant pads and similar ground works poured in cold conditions can use a calcium nitrate based set accelerator. It is chloride-free, which is why it is chosen where reinforcement corrosion is a concern.[1] The caution is cost in the other direction: higher accelerator doses raised shrinkage and early-age cracking risk in laboratory mortars.[2] Full detail is in our concrete accelerator guide.
  • Sulfide control in water systems. Nitrate dosing is an established way to stop sulfate-reducing bacteria from producing hydrogen sulfide in sewers and in oilfield injection water. Mine water and tailings water can host the same bacteria, but published dosing data for those systems is missing, so treat the sewer and oilfield figures as an analogy, not design data. See wastewater H2S control and oilfield souring control.

Uses sometimes claimed that we cannot support

You may see calcium nitrate described as a tailings dust suppressant, a way to immobilise heavy metals in tailings, or a way to prevent acid rock drainage. We searched for peer-reviewed support for each and did not find it. That is not proof that it does not work; it means there is no published evidence we can point you to, so HRSU does not market it for these uses. If you have your own trial data, we would like to see it.

How to evaluate calcium nitrate for a mining use

  1. State the objective. Faster set in cold weather, sulfide control in a water circuit, or something else.
  2. Get the data. For water: sulfate, sulfide, pH, temperature, residence time and metals. For concrete: cement type, temperature and your admixture limits.
  3. Request the TDS, SDS, COA and a sample and check the limits against your specification.
  4. Run a bench test, then a pilot, before any site-wide use.

Frequently asked questions

Does calcium nitrate suppress dust on tailings?

We found no published, peer-reviewed evidence for calcium nitrate as a tailings dust suppressant, and HRSU makes no such claim. Test any dust-control product on your own tailings before relying on it.

Can calcium nitrate prevent acid rock drainage?

We found no peer-reviewed evidence that calcium nitrate prevents acid rock drainage or immobilises heavy metals in tailings, so HRSU makes no such claim.
