Why Did Calcium Nitrate Prices Rise in October 2026?

Calcium nitrate is made by reacting nitric acid with limestone (calcium carbonate). Nitric acid is the largest single cost in the product, so a change in its price reaches the finished price within the same pricing cycle. In early October 2026, two major nitric acid producers in India revised their prices upward. The increase moved through the domestic market within days, and calcium nitrate manufacturers, HRSU included, reset their prices to match.

HRSU's October revision is 18.5% above the late-September price, measured on the same basis. It is a raw-material pass-through. It does not reflect a change in HRSU's grade, packing or terms.

How Is Nitric Acid Linked to Calcium Nitrate Cost?

The reaction is 2 HNO3 + CaCO3 → Ca(NO3)2 + H2O + CO2. By mass, one kilogram of anhydrous calcium nitrate (164.1 g/mol) consumes about 0.77 kg of nitric acid on a 100% basis (2 × 63.0 g/mol). Nitric acid therefore accounts for most of the material cost, while limestone is cheap and plentiful.

Nitric acid is in turn made from ammonia, which follows natural gas and energy prices. A calcium nitrate buyer is exposed to three steps of cost pass-through: gas, then ammonia, then nitric acid. That is why calcium nitrate prices tend to move in steps after upstream revisions rather than drift gradually.

What Does the HRSU Price Index Show for October?

The index is normalized to March 2026 = 100 and built from HRSU's order book, so it publishes direction and size of movement without disclosing individual prices. The October reading of 121.6 compares with 102.6 for September and 109.5 for August.

Read the series with its construction in mind. Month-to-month readings from the order book also reflect order size and delivery terms, such as whether freight is included in the rate. The October figure is cleaner than most: it is a list-price revision applied to the September baseline, so the 18.5% rise comes from raw-material cost alone.

How Should Procurement Teams Respond to the Price Increase?

Four actions apply to buyers of calcium nitrate for fertigation, concrete admixtures, wastewater treatment or mining use:

  • Confirm Q4 and Q1 volume now. Quotes issued before the revision will expire, and the market reprices quickly after a raw-material move.
  • Compare on a delivered basis. A lower ex-works rate with freight to-pay can cost more than a higher rate with freight included. Put every quote on the same incoterm before comparing.
  • Ask for the pricing formula. A quote linked to the published nitric acid price shows buyers where the next move comes from. A flat quote with no formula hides it.
  • Review contract tenor. If nitric acid eases later, fixed long-term prices lock in today's level. Quarterly reviews or a nitric-acid-indexed clause keep both sides aligned.

What Should Buyers Watch in November?

Three signals decide the next move: whether the October nitric acid increase holds through the month, the ammonia price trend that feeds the next nitric acid revision, and ocean freight rates for export cargo. HRSU will publish the November index in the first week of November.
