What Moved Calcium Nitrate Prices in October 2026?

In the first week of October, two major nitric acid producers in India raised their prices. Nitric acid is the largest material input in calcium nitrate, so the increase moved through the Indian market within days, and HRSU revised its own price over the first weekend of the month. Measured against the late-September price on the same basis, HRSU's revised price is 18.5% higher. That is a raw-material pass-through. Grade, packing and commercial terms did not change.

The revision matters beyond one month because of what it ended. In May, IMARC's forward view for nitric acid was "mostly steady" over the following three months, with a global benchmark near USD 0.36 per kg.14 That view did not hold in India. Buyers who budgeted Q4 on a flat nitric acid assumption are now exposed. The rest of this report explains why the acid moved, what the macro backdrop says about the next move, and what procurement teams should do before the November index.

Why Does Nitric Acid Drive Calcium Nitrate Cost?

Calcium nitrate is produced mainly by treating limestone with nitric acid: CaCO3 + 2 HNO3 → Ca(NO3)2 + CO2 + H2O.12 By mass, one kilogram of anhydrous calcium nitrate (164.1 g/mol) consumes about 0.77 kg of nitric acid on a 100% basis (2 × 63.0 g/mol). Limestone is cheap and plentiful, so nitric acid sets most of the material cost. A price move in the acid reaches the product almost one-for-one, adjusted only for the acid's share of total cost.

Nitric acid itself comes from ammonia. In the Ostwald process, ammonia is burned to nitric oxide, oxidized to nitrogen dioxide, and absorbed in water to form the acid, and the ammonia in turn comes from natural gas.13 The chain therefore runs: gas → ammonia → nitric acid → calcium nitrate. A buyer of calcium nitrate carries three layers of upstream price exposure, and each layer reprices on its own schedule. Finished calcium nitrate follows nitric acid in steps, which is why a pass-through shows up as a double-digit move in one month rather than a smooth drift.

Why Did Gas and Ammonia Costs Rise in 2026? The Macro Picture

Three macro forces explain the pressure on nitric acid. They are linked, but they work through different channels.

1. The Hormuz disruption hit the nitrogen chain directly

The conflict that began in February 2026 closed the Strait of Hormuz to most traffic. The World Bank reported that its fertilizer price index rose more than 12% in the first quarter and that urea climbed above USD 850 per tonne in April, up 80% since February.1 The Middle East accounts for nearly a quarter of global urea exports, so the disruption removed supply at the point where nitrogen trade is most concentrated.1 The International Trade Centre's analysis, published in August, puts numbers on the loss: exports of natural gas from Hormuz-dependent economies fell 95%, ammonia exports fell 75% and urea exports fell 83%.2 The strait carries about a quarter of seaborne oil trade and about a third of traded urea.2

For India the channel is concrete. Indian urea production depends on imported LNG, much of it from Qatar and the UAE through the same strait.5 When LNG is short, domestic ammonia economics tighten, and nitric acid producers pay more for feedstock. By August, ammonia in India was assessed at USD 445.49 per tonne CIF, against USD 354.70 per tonne FOB in China. The two prices are on different trade terms, so part of the gap is freight and insurance, but the direction is clear: Indian-delivered ammonia carries a premium. Procurement Resource attributes the firmness to geopolitical tension, higher LNG prices, freight charges and constrained import availability.6

2. Oil and energy prices keep input costs elevated

Brent traded near USD 98 a barrel on 6 October, up about 1.4% over the month.15 In August, Brent was up around 24% since the war began, with the strait still largely closed: about 10 vessels crossed on one day in August against roughly 130 daily transits before the conflict.10 The U.S. EIA's September outlook sets Brent at about USD 90 for the second half of 2026, USD 8 above the prior month's forecast, and assumes Middle East oil flows stay constrained through the fourth quarter.3 Energy is the marginal cost of ammonia, so a Brent price that stays near USD 100 keeps gas-linked feedstock costs high even when physical LNG flows improve.

3. A weak rupee and tighter money raise the rupee cost of imported inputs

USD/INR stood at 96.56 on 6 October, with the Reserve Bank of India selling dollars to slow the decline and Indian companies recording a record USD 77 billion of currency hedges in September.8 India imports LNG, ammonia and a large share of its energy in dollars. A weaker rupee lifts the rupee cost of every one of those inputs, so Indian producers raise rupee prices even when dollar benchmarks are flat. Retail inflation reached 4.82% in August, and economists expect the RBI's October 7 decision to deliver a 25 basis point hike to 5.50%, the first since February 2023.9 A rate hike raises carrying costs for chemical distributors and manufacturers, which discourages inventory holding and pushes buying into shorter, more frequent orders.

Why now, if dollar ammonia eased in July?

The question is fair. Green Gubre Group's July report shows Middle East ammonia FOB falling from USD 680 to USD 560 per tonne and East Asia CFR falling by about USD 90 over the month, while urea rose.7 Our assessment is that three things kept that relief from reaching Indian nitric acid. Indian-delivered ammonia was still USD 445 per tonne CIF in August.6 The rupee kept weakening against the dollar.8 And producers price nitric acid from contract and inventory cost, so a spot-market dip passes through with a delay while a sustained cost increase gets revised in one step. October is when that step arrived.

Is the Supply Situation Improving?

Partly. September brought the first real relief. S&P Global Energy counted 19 LNG cargoes through Hormuz in September, 13 from Qatar and six from the UAE, and Kpler recorded 21 cargoes leaving in September against 15 in June, the highest monthly level since the war began.5 Saudi Aramco cut its November Arab Light price to Asian buyers to USD 5 below the regional benchmark, a deeper discount than the previous month's USD 2, which signals that crude is competing for market share again.15

The data supports a cautious reading. The EIA still assumes constrained Middle East flows through the fourth quarter and expects most production and trade flows to return to pre-conflict averages only by the second quarter of 2027.3 Even then, some Persian Gulf producers will not reach pre-war output during the forecast period.3 Easing at the LNG and crude level reaches ammonia and nitric acid with a lag, and producers who just repriced upward do not reverse within weeks. Price declines in chemical chains move slower than price increases.

What Does the HRSU Price Index Show?

HRSU's index is built from our own ex-works order book and normalized to March 2026 = 100, so it shows direction and percentage change without disclosing any individual price. October reads 121.6, against 102.6 for September and 109.5 for August. Read the series with its construction in mind. The monthly figure averages the orders placed that month, so it reflects order mix and delivery terms (for example, whether freight is included in the rate) as well as the underlying price. The September reading sits below August partly for that reason, because the delivery terms differed between the two months.

The October reading is cleaner than most, because it is a price revision applied to the September baseline. The 18.5% move is raw-material pass-through. The earlier swings in the series, including the April high of 150, are order-book readings and should be compared with care. HRSU publishes the index monthly, and the series has a gap (there is no May reading) where no qualifying order fell in the month.

What Is Our Forward Guidance for November 2026 to March 2027?

We frame the outlook as three scenarios, each with the trigger that decides it. This is HRSU's own judgment and not a model forecast.

Base case: the October level holds through December

Hormuz traffic keeps recovering gradually, as September's LNG numbers suggest, but the EIA's constrained-flow assumption for the fourth quarter proves broadly right.3 Oil stays between USD 90 and USD 100. Nitric acid producers hold their October revision, and calcium nitrate prices stay flat to slightly firm through India's rabi season. Under this case, expect no price relief before the first quarter of 2027, and HRSU's November index should read within a few points of October.

Upside risk: a second step up

Diplomacy stalls, attacks on shipping resume (the August strikes in the Bab al-Mandeb strait already showed that risk),10 and LNG cargoes fall back from September's war-time high.5 In that case ammonia reprices upward again, nitric acid follows, and a second calcium nitrate revision before year-end becomes likely. A rate hike and a rupee near or beyond 97 add to this pressure.89

Downside risk: early relief

The strait reopens on a durable basis, Brent moves toward the EIA's 2027 path of USD 77 in the second quarter and USD 67 in the second half,3 and nitric acid producers start rolling prices back in December or January. Even here, expect a partial reversal. Producers who absorbed higher gas costs for eight months will recover margin before they cut.

The longer structural story is separate from this cycle. India has contracted 724,000 tonnes a year of green ammonia for 13 fertilizer units under the SIGHT programme, but the first deliveries are not expected before 2028, with bulk volumes in 2029–2030.11 That supply will reduce India's gas exposure, but it does not reach the 2026–27 price cycle.

How Should Procurement Teams Respond to the October Increase?

Seven actions follow from the analysis above. They apply to buyers of calcium nitrate for fertigation, concrete admixtures, wastewater treatment and mining use.

  1. Cover Q4 and Q1 volume now. Our base case has no relief before 2027, and the upside scenario has a second step. As a rule of thumb, locking 60–70% of expected volume at the current level caps the upside risk and leaves the rest free to float if the downside scenario plays out.
  2. Compare quotes on a delivered basis. A lower ex-works rate with freight to-pay can cost more than a higher rate with freight included. Put every quote on the same incoterm before comparing. Drewry's composite stood at USD 4,434 per 40-foot container on 1 October, down 1% on the week, with Shanghai–Los Angeles at USD 7,835 and Shanghai–Rotterdam at USD 3,399.4 Those are China-origin lanes. India-origin lanes follow the same direction with their own premiums, so ask your forwarder for current Nhava Sheva and Mundra quotes rather than reading across.
  3. Ask suppliers for the pricing formula. A quote tied to a published nitric acid reference shows where the next move comes from. A flat quote with no formula hides it, and it usually expires early.
  4. Use short tenors with a review clause. A fixed 12-month price locks in a level set at the peak of a supply shock. Quarterly resets or a nitric-acid-indexed clause keep both sides aligned if the downside scenario plays out.
  5. Dual-source and check route exposure. The Bab al-Mandeb attacks in August show that shipping routes remain exposed.10 Buyers in Europe and the Middle East should confirm routing and insurance terms with every supplier, including HRSU.
  6. Track freight and currency separately. A weak rupee makes India-origin goods cheaper in dollar terms, while Indian producers add rupee cost for dollar-priced inputs.8 The two effects pull in opposite directions, so review the net effect each month rather than assuming either one wins.
  7. Keep safety stock one cycle larger than normal. Gulf logistics remain fragmented and origin-specific.7 A single delayed cargo costs more than a few weeks of extra carrying cost.

What Should Buyers Watch Before the November Index?

Five indicators decide the next move. First, Hormuz transit counts and monthly LNG cargo numbers; a second month near September's 19 to 21 cargoes would confirm the recovery.5 Second, the Brent path against USD 90 and USD 100.3 Third, the October 7 RBI decision and the rupee's reaction.9 Fourth, any further nitric acid revision announced for November. Fifth, Drewry's weekly index, which on 1 October described East–West container freight as under pressure as China's Golden Week began.4 HRSU will publish the November index in the first week of November and will state each driver's movement against this report.
