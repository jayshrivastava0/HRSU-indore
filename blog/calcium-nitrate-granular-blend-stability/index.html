Understanding Calcium Nitrate’s Stability for Optimized Plant Nutrition

Calcium Nitrate’s stability is a critical factor in optimizing plant nutrition, particularly for high-value crops within the United States agricultural sector. Understanding its characteristics—specifically its solubility and potential degradation pathways—allows procurement professionals to select the most cost-effective and reliable fertilizer source. Calcium Nitrate (15.5%N+ 19.0% Calcium) offers a significant advantage due to its dual-nutrient contribution: readily available nitrogen for vegetative growth and a substantial dose of calcium, essential for cell wall development, fruit set, and overall plant health4. Its high water solubility, around 1:16, facilitates efficient nutrient uptake, particularly beneficial in intensive American horticultural operations. However, maintaining this solubility, and thus the fertilizer’s efficacy, hinges on careful handling and storage conditions. Procurement of Calcium Nitrate must account for factors impacting its stability. While generally stable in dry conditions, Calcium Nitrate solutions are susceptible to degradation when exposed to prolonged periods of sunlight or elevated temperatures. This degradation manifests primarily as nitrate loss, diminishing the nitrogen available to the plant. Furthermore, the presence of moisture accelerates this process . Maintaining a consistent particle size, ideally a fine granular form, also maximizes surface area and improves dissolution rates, furthering its stability. Evaluating Calcium Nitrate’s Hygroscopicity and Moisture Content

Calcium Nitrate’s hygroscopicity and moisture content represent critical considerations during procurement for American horticultural operations, particularly those focused on high-value crops like carrots, cucurbit vegetables, and fruiting vegetables1. Understanding these properties is vital for ensuring accurate dosage, effective fertigation, and ultimately, maximized yield. Calcium Nitrate (15.5%N+ 19.0% Calcium) exhibits high solubility, typically dissolving at a rate of approximately 1:1 in water6, but its inherent hygroscopicity – its tendency to absorb moisture from the air – significantly impacts its handling and storage. Excess moisture within the fertilizer granules will lead to diminished nutrient availability, potentially reducing the efficacy of calcium delivery to the plant’s root system.
