What is calcium nitrate used for? Every major use, explained with sources

Last updated: 7 October 2026  ·  Reviewed by Sujay Shrivastava - Digital Transformation Consultant
In summary

Calcium nitrate, Ca(NO3)2, is used mainly as a water-soluble fertilizer that supplies calcium and nitrate-nitrogen (typically 15.5-0-0 plus about 19% calcium),[27] for soil, drip fertigation, hydroponics and foliar sprays against calcium-deficiency disorders such as bitter pit and blossom-end rot.[3][6][8] Its industrial uses include a set accelerator and corrosion-related admixture in concrete,[15][16] sulfide and odour control in sewers and oilfield waters,[17][18][19] a latex coagulant in glove dipping,[9][10] a minor component of molten-salt heat-storage fluids,[11] and niche roles in deicing, lake-sediment treatment and heat-storage materials.[14][20][22] It is also a regulated oxidising solid, so storage and handling rules apply.[2]

Which uses are mainstream and which are niche?

Mainstream, high volume: agriculture (soil, fertigation, hydroponics, foliar sprays), where the combination of soluble calcium and nitrate nitrogen is the reason to choose it over other nitrogen sources.[3][27] Established industrial: concrete admixtures,[15] sewer sulfide control,[17][18] oilfield souring control[19] and latex coagulation.[10] Niche or developing: molten-salt blends,[11] deicing,[14] lake-sediment treatment[20] and phase-change heat storage.[22] Be sceptical of any supplier page that presents niche uses as routine; the dedicated guides above state the evidence and limits for each.

How is calcium nitrate made, and what forms exist?

Industrially it is made by reacting limestone or lime with nitric acid, or recovered as a by-product of the nitrophosphate (Odda) process, then evaporated and finished as granules, prills, flakes or powder.[24][25] It is sold as the tetrahydrate, as anhydrous material, or as a calcium-ammonium-nitrate double salt (the form behind many “CAN” style products).[27] Form matters: fertigation wants fully soluble powder, concrete and latex users want controlled impurities, and heat-storage work wants low-water material. Is calcium nitrate dangerous?

It is an oxidising solid (UN 1454, class 5.1, packing group III) that strengthens fires and must be kept apart from combustible and reducing materials; ingestion can cause methaemoglobinaemia.[2][26] It is not the same substance as ammonium nitrate. Storage, spill and transport rules are covered in safety, handling and storage. HRSU gives no information on explosive use; any such use is regulated and outside this guide.

When calcium nitrate is NOT the right choice

  • As a substitute where chloride-based or other specialised products are specified. Several uses above are niche, and a specification, not a catalogue listing, should decide the material.[14][16]
  • Mixing concentrated calcium nitrate with sulfate or phosphate stock solutions. They precipitate; keep them in separate tanks.[3]
  • Storing it near fuels, organics or reducing agents. It is an oxidiser and intensifies fires.[2]

Frequently asked questions

What is calcium nitrate used for?

Mainly as a soluble calcium and nitrate-nitrogen fertilizer for soil, fertigation, hydroponics and foliar sprays. Industrial uses include concrete admixtures, sewer and oilfield sulfide control, latex coagulation in glove dipping, and minor roles in molten-salt storage, deicing and sediment treatment.[3][15][17][19][10][11][27]

What is the main use of calcium nitrate?

Agriculture. It supplies calcium and nitrate-nitrogen together in a fully water-soluble form; the common fertilizer grade is 15.5-0-0 with about 19% calcium.[27][3]

Is calcium nitrate used in concrete?

Yes, as a chloride-free set-accelerating admixture, and it has been studied as a steel-corrosion inhibitor, with mixed results at lower doses.[15][16]

Is calcium nitrate used for odour control in sewage?

Yes. Nitrate dosing in force mains and lift stations prevents sulfide formation, which reduces hydrogen sulfide odour and corrosion.[17][18]

How is calcium nitrate used in rubber gloves?

As the coagulant in coagulant dipping: a former is dipped in a calcium nitrate solution (patent examples cite 250–300 g/L), dried, then dipped in latex so the latex coagulates onto it.[9][10]

Is calcium nitrate used in deicing?

It has been studied as a granular, low-corrosion deicer, but it is niche compared with sodium, calcium and magnesium chloride, acetates and formates.[13][14]

Is calcium nitrate the same as ammonium nitrate or CAN?

No. Calcium nitrate is Ca(NO3)2. Calcium ammonium nitrate (CAN) is a different, double-salt product that contains ammonium nitrogen, and ammonium nitrate is a separately regulated oxidiser.[27][2]

Is calcium nitrate dangerous or explosive?

It is an oxidising solid (UN 1454, class 5.1) that intensifies fires, so it must be stored away from combustibles. Ingestion can cause methaemoglobinaemia. See the safety guide for handling rules.[2][26]

Which calcium nitrate grade should I buy?

Fertigation and hydroponics need fully water-soluble powder; industrial users should define limits for impurities and moisture in writing and trial a sample first. HRSU supplies fertilizer-grade powder with a batch COA.
